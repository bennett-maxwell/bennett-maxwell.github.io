Territory — what the FDD says (Item 12)

Protected territory — classified from Item 12, p.18 of the brand's FDD

Under this brand's Item 12 rule, the territory carries some protection but not full exclusivity — for example, the franchisor may reserve certain channels, accounts, or future concepts. Areas inside an existing franchisee's protected territory are generally not available to a new buyer for a directly competing unit; the exact limits are in the FDD text quoted above.

The FDD's own words (Item 12): “territory is NOT exclusive - the FDD states you will not receive an exclusive territory.”

What is NOT available — and where availability is most plausible

Not available: any area inside an existing franchisee's protected territory while that agreement runs. Existing units sit in: AZ, CA, CO, CT, DE, FL, GA, IL, IN, LA, MD, MO, NE, NJ, NM, NC, OH, OR, PA, SC, TX, VA, WA — a specific address near an existing unit is governed by that territory's boundaries.

Most plausible availability (inference, not a promise): the FDD discloses no existing units and no projected openings in AL, AK, AR, DC, HI, ID, IA, KS, KY, ME, MA, MI, MN, MS, MT, NV, NH, NY, ND, OK, RI, SD, TN, UT, VT, WV, WI, WY. Combined with the Item 12 rule above, these are the areas where availability is most plausible.
