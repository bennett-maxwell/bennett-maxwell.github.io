Best Franchises to Buy in 2027: A Criteria-Based Guide (Not a Sales List)

Why most "best franchises" lists mislead you

Most best-franchise listicles rank whoever paid for placement, quote the franchise fee as if it were the cost, and copy figures from last year's FDD. A franchise decision is typically a 5–10 year contract and a six-figure commitment. It deserves a better method.

This guide uses five criteria you can verify yourself, in the brand's own Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD):

  1. Total initial investment (FDD Item 7) — the low-to-high range to open, not the fee alone.
  2. Financial performance disclosure (FDD Item 19) — does the brand disclose anything at all, and on what basis? Company-owned results are not franchisee results; the label matters.
  3. Owner role — owner-operator, semi-absentee, or manager-run. The best business on paper fails if the daily role doesn't fit your life.
  4. Inventory and labour complexity — what do you have to buy, store, staff, and manage every week?
  5. System health (FDD Item 20) — openings, closures, and transfers over three years, plus the franchisee contact list you should actually call.

How to use this list

Each entry below states who the concept genuinely fits, its Item 7 range where FKI holds the FDD of record, its Item 19 basis where disclosed, and the honest trade-off. If a brand you are considering won't give you its FDD early, that is your answer about that brand.

The field, by buyer type

1. For the hands-on food operator: Salad House (fast-casual salads & bowls)

  • What it is: A New Jersey-born fast-casual concept — made-to-order salads, grain bowls, wraps, sandwiches, soups and smoothies — franchising since 2017.
  • Total initial investment (Item 7, FDD of record): $303,700–$750,500, including a $40,000 initial franchise fee.
  • Item 19 — read the label: the disclosed average of $1,612,384 and top figure of $2,269,241 are company-owned store results. They show what the concept can do under the founder's own operation; they are not a promise of franchisee results, and any salesperson who presents them without the "company-owned" label is misrepresenting them.
  • Owner role: hands-on. Food rewards operators who are in the store, especially in year one.
  • Trade-off: real estate, build-out and staffing complexity are the highest on this list. The Item 7 range is wide because site and build-out choices drive it — budget toward the high end and validate with current franchisees from the Item 20 list.

2. For the retail operator who hates inventory risk: Indy Clover (booth-rental thrift & consignment)

  • What it is: A women's thrift and consignment store with a flipped model: local sellers rent booths, bring their own items, set their own prices, and keep two-thirds of their sales. The franchisee collects booth rent plus one-third of sales — without buying or managing the inventory sellers bring.
  • Total initial investment (2026 FDD, Item 7): $174,450–$225,800, including the $35,000 franchise fee (plus a $5,000 training fee and an $80,000 opening order inside that range).
  • Item 19 — a different, more conservative basis: the 2026 FDD reports performance on a Post-Seller-Profit basis: average Post-Seller-Profit of $303,604, with average estimated earnings of $68,129 (22.4%). Note how different that framing is from a gross-sales headline — it is closer to what an owner actually keeps, and it is the right way to compare.
  • Owner role: owner-operator or semi-absentee with a strong local social-media presence; the model runs on fresh booths and community sellers.
  • Trade-off: territory is non-exclusive (Item 12) — you are buying a system and a head start, not a protected monopoly. Your moat is execution and community, not a map line.

3. For the home-services buyer who wants no retail build-out: Molly Maid (residential cleaning)

  • What it is: A residential home-cleaning franchise in the Neighborly system, franchising in the US since 1984 — scheduled, recurring cleaning visits delivered by employed teams, run from a small office rather than a retail storefront. (Category and franchising-since facts: Franchise Decision Radar's Molly Maid review, summarising the brand's FDD filings — https://franchisedecisionradar.com/molly-maid.html)
  • Total initial investment (secondary source — verify against the current FDD): a SharpSheets summary of the Molly Maid FDD puts the total estimated initial investment at $144,150–$203,950, including a $14,900 initial franchise fee plus a separate territory fee. FKI does not hold this brand's FDD of record, so treat that range as a sourced starting point, not a quote — the binding figure is Item 7 of the current FDD the franchisor gives you. (Source: https://sharpsheets.io/blog/molly-maid-franchise-fdd-profits-costs/)
  • Owner role: executive / manager-run in most markets — hiring, scheduling and quality control, not cleaning houses yourself.
  • Trade-off: service concepts occupy the lower Item 7 ranges in franchising because there is no retail build-out — but a low Item 7 usually means the owner is the sales engine. If you will not personally sell and network in year one, the low price is a trap, not a bargain. Read Item 20 closures carefully — churn is real in this tier — and call former franchisees, not just current ones. FKI is not compensated by this brand; it is included as a genuine non-represented alternative.

4. For the hands-on services operator: TWO MEN AND A TRUCK (moving & storage)

  • What it is: A franchised moving company — local and long-distance moves, packing and storage — franchising since 1989, built around trucks, crews and local reputation rather than a consumer storefront. (Category facts: 1851 Franchise's TWO MEN AND A TRUCK cost/fees deep-dive — source below.)
  • Total initial investment (secondary source — verify against the current FDD): 1851 Franchise's breakdown of the brand's 2025 FDD puts the total at $92,100–$247,500 for a Mod Market territory and $146,950–$506,450 for a Metro Market territory — the same brand prices very differently by territory size, which is exactly why a single headline number misleads. (Source: https://1851franchise.com/two-men-and-a-truck-franchise-costs-fees-profit-and-data-for-2025-2730007)
  • Owner role: operator-led — crew hiring, truck utilisation and local referral relationships are the business.
  • Trade-off: labour and vehicle costs are the daily fight, and demand is seasonal. The criteria that decide this one are Item 20 system health in your territory size and whether the territory population supports enough move volume year-round. FKI is not compensated by this brand; it is included as a genuine non-represented alternative.

5. For the semi-absentee investor: Anytime Fitness (24-hour fitness clubs)

  • What it is: A 24/7-access gym franchise — smaller-format clubs with key-fob member access and a lean staff, under Purpose Brands. (Category facts: Franchise Chatter's Anytime Fitness review — source below.)
  • Total initial investment (secondary source — verify against the current FDD): Franchise Chatter's Item-by-Item breakdown, stated as based on Item 7 of the brand's 2026 FDD, puts the total estimated initial investment at $539,329–$905,482, including a $42,500 initial franchise/development fee. (Source: https://www.franchisechatter.com/2026/08/11/anytime-fitness-franchise-review-2026-costs-fees-news-average-revenues-and-or-profits/)
  • Owner role: the classic membership-model semi-absentee pitch — a manager runs the floor. Test it, don't assume it.
  • Trade-off: membership-revenue concepts attract semi-absentee buyers, but two checks matter more than the brochure: (1) Item 19 basis — membership pre-sales flatter early numbers; (2) the manager labour line in your own pro forma — a semi-absentee model that only works with an underpaid manager doesn't work. Build your model at the 40th percentile of disclosed performance, not the average. FKI is not compensated by this brand; it is included as a genuine non-represented alternative.

6. For the multi-unit food builder: Jersey Mike's Subs (sandwich QSR)

  • What it is: A made-to-order submarine-sandwich quick-service restaurant chain, franchising since 1987 — the established national food benchmark on this list, against which smaller food concepts like Salad House should honestly be compared. (Category facts: Franchise Direct's Jersey Mike's cost/fees summary — source below.)
  • Total initial investment (secondary source — verify against the current FDD): Franchise Direct's Item 7 table puts the estimated total at $436,176–$1,162,228, including a $20,000 initial franchise fee and a $10,000 development fee — the widest range on this list, driven by leasehold improvements and site choice. (Source: https://www.franchisedirect.com/jersey-mikes-franchise-costs-fees)
  • Owner role: hands-on or area-developer; food at this scale is an operating system, not a passive asset.
  • Trade-off: if your plan is three-plus units, evaluate the system, not the unit: Item 20 growth trend, Item 21 franchisor financials (can they support you in year three?), and whether the development schedule in the addendum matches your capital. The discipline is to make the franchisor's timeline and your financing timeline the same document before you sign. FKI is not compensated by this brand; it is included as a genuine non-represented alternative.

The 7-step way to buy any franchise on this list

  1. Write your profile first: capital, role, territory, timeline — before you look at brands.
  2. Build a shortlist of 3–5 concepts that fit the profile, not 30 that don't.
  3. Get each FDD and use the mandatory 14-day window properly.
  4. Read Items 7, 19 and 20 first; Items 3, 12 and 17 second.
  5. Call 10–15 franchisees from Item 20 — including people who left.
  6. Build your pro forma at conservative (40th-percentile) performance, with owner pay as a cost line.
  7. Have a franchise attorney and an accountant review your deal before you sign — there is no federal right to cancel afterwards.

FAQ

What is the best franchise to buy in 2027? There isn't one — there is a best fit for a specific buyer, budget, role and territory. Any list that names a single winner without asking those four questions is selling, not advising.

How much does it cost to open a franchise? The only figure that binds a brand is the Item 7 total initial investment in its current FDD. On this list, the ranges quoted run from the Mod Market range for TWO MEN AND A TRUCK ($92,100–$247,500, secondary summary of its 2025 FDD) up to Jersey Mike's Subs ($436,176–$1,162,228, secondary Item 7 summary). The two ranges FKI quotes from FDDs it holds are $174,450–$225,800 (Indy Clover, 2026 FDD) and $303,700–$750,500 (Salad House, FDD of record). Home-services concepts can start lower; full restaurants can run far higher.

What does Item 19 actually tell me? Item 19 is the only place a franchisor may legally make an earnings claim, and disclosing one is optional. When figures appear, the footnotes define everything: which outlets, which period, and which costs are excluded. Salad House's figures are company-owned results; Indy Clover's 2026 figures are stated on a Post-Seller-Profit basis. Comparing the two headline numbers directly would be a category error.

Is a franchise consultant free? Consulting is typically free to the buyer because the franchisor pays a referral fee when a placed buyer signs. That is also a disclosed incentive — ask any consultant, including us, which brands they are compensated on.
