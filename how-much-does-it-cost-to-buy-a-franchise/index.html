How Much Does It Cost to Open a Franchise in 2027?

What is the short answer?

The cost to open a franchise is the total initial investment in Item 7 of the brand's Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) — never the franchise fee alone.

For the two concepts used as worked examples in this article, the current FDD ranges are:

  • Indy Clover (thrift & consignment retail, 2026 FDD): $174,450–$225,800 total, including a $35,000 franchise fee.
  • Salad House (fast-casual restaurant, FDD of record): $303,700–$750,500 total, including a $40,000 franchise fee.

Home-based service concepts can start below those ranges. Full-service restaurants and hotels can exceed $1 million. The fee alone is typically a small fraction of the total.

Why is the franchise fee not the cost?

The franchise fee buys the licence, training and onboarding. It does not buy the building work, equipment, signage, opening inventory, deposits, professional fees, or the cash you need to survive the first months.

Item 7 exists precisely because buyers kept being misled by fee-only advertising. By law, it estimates everything required to open and operate through the initial period — and it is the only cost figure a brand is bound to disclose in that standardised form.

What does Item 7 include?

Item 7 is a line-by-line table. Typical lines are:

  • Initial franchise fee (Item 5)
  • Training travel and living expenses
  • Real-estate deposits and first rent payments
  • Leasehold improvements / build-out
  • Equipment, furniture, fixtures and signage
  • Opening inventory and supplies
  • Insurance, licences and professional fees
  • Grand-opening marketing
  • Additional funds — working capital for the first months

Two worked examples show how differently the same table can look. Indy Clover's 2026 Item 7 includes a $5,000 training fee and an $80,000 opening order, because a retail store's money goes into the floor set-up and stock presentation. Salad House's range is far wider — $303,700 to $750,500 — because restaurant build-out and site costs vary enormously by location. A wide Item 7 range is not evasiveness; it is the brand telling you that your site choice is your biggest cost decision.

What will you pay after opening?

Opening cost is only the first number. Item 6 of the FDD lists every recurring fee: royalty (usually a percentage of gross sales), brand/marketing fund contributions, technology fees, and renewal and transfer fees.

Before you sign, add Item 6 fees to your pro forma as a percentage of conservative revenue — not the brand's average. A model that only works at average performance is a plan to be a below-average owner with above-average stress.

How much cash do you actually need?

Lenders and franchisors look at liquid capital separately from net worth. You need enough liquid capital to cover your share of the Item 7 total after financing, plus personal living expenses through the ramp.

A practical rule used by franchise consultants: budget to the high end of the Item 7 range, hold living expenses outside the business budget, and never let a salesperson re-anchor you to the low end or the fee.

How can you verify any cost figure you are quoted?

Use this three-step check on any number anyone gives you — including numbers in this article:

  1. Ask which FDD, and which Item. A legal earnings or cost claim must trace to the current FDD. Cost = Item 7. Fees = Items 5 and 6.
  2. Check the basis. Is a performance figure an average of all outlets, a subset, company-owned stores, or franchisee stores? Salad House's Item 19 average of $1,612,384 (top store $2,269,241) is company-owned performance. Indy Clover's 2026 Item 19 takes a different approach entirely, reporting average Post-Seller-Profit of $303,604 and average estimated earnings of $68,129 (22.4%). Those two numbers cannot be compared headline-to-headline — the basis is the story.
  3. Use the 14 days. A franchisor must give you the FDD at least 14 calendar days before you sign or pay anything. Spend them with a franchise attorney, an accountant, and phone calls to current and former franchisees listed in Item 20.

What is the most common cost mistake?

Underestimating in three places: build-out (it runs over), working capital (the ramp is slower than the brochure), and owner pay (forgetting to pay yourself in the model, then discovering the business "works" only if you work free).

The second most common mistake is comparing brands by franchise fee. A $35,000 fee inside a $174,450–$225,800 total (Indy Clover) and a $40,000 fee inside a $303,700–$750,500 total (Salad House) are not $5,000 apart. They are different businesses with different totals.

FAQ

How much does it cost to open a franchise in 2027?

It depends on the concept, and the binding figure is Item 7 of that brand's current FDD. Worked examples from current FDDs: $174,450–$225,800 (Indy Clover, 2026 FDD) and $303,700–$750,500 (Salad House, FDD of record). Always confirm against the FDD issued for the year you sign.

What is the franchise fee for Salad House and Indy Clover?

Salad House: $40,000 initial franchise fee, inside a $303,700–$750,500 total initial investment. Indy Clover: $35,000 initial franchise fee (plus a $5,000 training fee), inside a $174,450–$225,800 total initial investment per its 2026 FDD.

Can I open a franchise for under $100,000?

Some home-based and mobile service concepts disclose Item 7 totals under $100,000. Read Item 20 (closures and transfers) especially carefully in that tier, and be honest about whether you will personally do the selling the model assumes.

What financing do franchise buyers use?

Common routes are SBA 7(a) loans, conventional lenders, and ROBS retirement-fund rollovers. Each has different down-payment, collateral and guarantee terms — a franchise consultant can introduce specialist lenders, but the lending decision is always the lender's.

Is a franchise consultant free?

Typically yes for the buyer: the franchisor pays the consultant a referral fee if a buyer they introduced signs. Franchise KI works on that model, and this article discloses it because the incentive matters: verify every figure in the FDD yourself, whoever introduced you to the brand.
